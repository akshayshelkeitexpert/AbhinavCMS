Railway HVAC Compressor Testing Facility – Hinjewadi, Pune, Maharashtra

Location: Hinjawadi, Pune, Maharashtra. Project Type: Industrial Railway HVAC Compressor Testing Facility. Operational Requirement: 24 × 7 Continuous Testing Operation. Design Temperature: 23°C NMT (Not More Than 23°C). Critical Air Velocity at Testing Rig: 1–2 m/s.

Design Temperature23°C NMT
Air Velocity1–2 m/s at testing rig

Project Engineering Objective

The primary objective was to engineer a high-precision industrial HVAC system capable of delivering:

  • Controlled airflow velocity (1–2 m/s) at compressor testing rigs
  • Stable ambient temperature ≤23°C under dynamic heat loads
  • Uniform airflow distribution without turbulence or stratification
  • Continuous-duty reliability for 24/7 industrial testing

Unlike conventional comfort cooling, this project required airflow performance engineering to meet strict velocity and environmental stability parameters critical for railway HVAC compressor validation.

Airflow Engineering & System Design Approach

1. High-Capacity Air Handling System

  • 2 Nos. Industrial AHUs
  • Capacity: 45,000 CFM each
  • Total Airflow: 90,000 CFM
  • Designed for high static pressure handling
  • Continuous-duty blowers with optimized fan curves for airflow stability

The AHUs were selected based on airflow volume, pressure drop calculations, and velocity control requirements at the end-use testing zone.

2. Precision Cooling Integration

The AHUs were integrated with Daikin VRV (Variable Refrigerant Volume) System, enabling:

  • Accurate refrigerant modulation
  • Stable supply air temperature control
  • Quick response to fluctuating heat loads generated during compressor testing
  • Energy-efficient part-load performance

The system was engineered to maintain ≤23°C NMT even under peak operational conditions.

3. Air Distribution & Velocity Control Engineering

Achieving the required 1–2 meters per second air velocity at the testing rig was the core technical challenge.

Key engineering measures implemented:

  • Detailed airflow modeling and duct sizing calculations
  • Optimized duct cross-sections to maintain controlled air velocity
  • Strategic outlet positioning for laminar airflow delivery
  • Static pressure balancing across the distribution network
  • Minimization of turbulence at the testing zone

The ducting system included:

  • GI ducting for outdoor routing and structural durability
  • PIR insulated ducting for indoor sections to minimize thermal losses and condensation

Special emphasis was placed on maintaining velocity control without excessive noise or vibration, ensuring test accuracy.

Technical Challenges Addressed

Controlled Air Velocity (1–2 m/s)

Maintaining uniform airflow velocity at the compressor testing rig required precise balancing between: air quantity (CFM), duct geometry, static pressure, outlet configuration.

Temperature Stability ≤23°C (NMT)

  • Continuous monitoring of supply air conditions
  • Heat load compensation during live compressor testing
  • Prevention of thermal stratification within large-volume space

24/7 Industrial Reliability

  • Heavy-duty motors and components
  • Continuous operational stability
  • Industrial-grade insulation and sealing

Engineering Outcome

The completed system delivers:

  • Stable, repeatable airflow conditions for railway HVAC compressor validation
  • Controlled air velocity of 1–2 m/s at critical testing zones
  • Precise temperature maintenance ≤23°C under dynamic load
  • Uniform air distribution without turbulence
  • Reliable continuous operation suitable for industrial R&D environments
